# Cold Email Drafting

Write cold outreach emails that get replies instead of deletions. Built from
real campaigns: 44 local businesses emailed across 6 waves, ~30 cold calls,
and the hard lessons that came out of them. The core insight: sell the
outcome, never the mechanism.

---

## The 3 rules that matter most

1. **Sell the outcome, never the tool.** Don't lead with "AI", "automation",
   "software", or any mechanism. Lead with the pain disappearing: missed
   calls, cold quotes, no-shows, empty chairs. Reveal the *how* only when
   asked.
2. **One email, one ask.** Every cold email ends in a single low-friction
   call to action. Never "reply or call or book or check the site".
3. **Make the first line undeniable.** The preview line decides whether the
   email gets opened. Reference something specific about *their* business —
   a review, a location, a recent post. Generic flattery is worse than none.

## Anatomy of a cold email that works

```
Subject:  3-7 words, specific, no clickbait
Preview:  First line visible in inbox — make it personal
Body:     Hook (their pain) → Proof (one line) → Offer (free/low-risk) → Ask (one CTA)
Sign-off: Name + one contact method
```

Keep the body under 120 words. If it scrolls on a phone, it's too long.

## Subject lines

Good patterns:
- Question about a specific pain: "Quick question about missed calls at {shop}"
- Plain and direct: "15-min idea for {business name}"
- Reference trigger: "Saw your review about {thing}"

Bad patterns (never use):
- "Revolutionize your business with AI"
- "Quick question" (alone — overused)
- Anything with !!!, ALL CAPS, or "free $$$"

## The body formula: Hook → Proof → Offer → Ask

**Hook (1-2 sentences).** Name a pain they feel weekly. Be concrete:
> "Most shops I talk to lose 2-3 jobs a month to calls that go to voicemail
> after hours."

Not: "In today's competitive landscape, businesses need digital solutions."

**Proof (1 sentence).** One specific, believable claim. Small and true beats
big and vague:
> "We set this up for a shop in [town] and they caught 11 after-hours calls
> in the first month."

Never invent numbers. If you have no proof yet, say what the pilot measures
instead of claiming results.

**Offer (1-2 sentences).** Make it free and time-boxed. The free pilot
framing lowers the ask to almost zero:
> "I'm running a free 14-day pilot for 3 local shops — no card, no contract.
> If it doesn't pay for itself you keep the setup notes either way."

**Ask (1 sentence).** One CTA, low friction:
> "Worth a 10-minute call Tuesday or Thursday?"

Not: "Let me know a time that works for you or feel free to check out our
website or reply with questions."

## Personalization that actually scales

Tier your effort. 80% of replies come from the top tier:

- **Tier 1 (5 min each):** Reference a specific review, their busiest hours,
  a service they clearly offer, or something from their site/socials.
- **Tier 2 (1 min each):** Industry + town + one plausible pain point.
- **Tier 3 (bulk):** Industry-level pain only. Expect low reply rates; use
  only for list-building, not for your best prospects.

## The free-pilot email (full template)

```
Subject: missed calls at {business}?

Hi {name},

Noticed {business} closes at {time} — most {industry} shops I talk to lose
a few jobs a month to after-hours calls that hit voicemail and never call
back.

I'm running a free 14-day pilot for 3 local {industry} shops: every call
gets answered, quotes go out same-night, nothing falls through. No card,
no contract — if it doesn't pay for itself you keep the setup notes.

Worth a 10-minute call Tuesday or Thursday?

{your name}
{phone}
```

## Follow-up sequence

Most replies come from follow-ups 2 and 3, not email 1. Never send more
than 4 touches.

- **Touch 1 (day 0):** The cold email above.
- **Touch 2 (day 3):** One-line bump. "Bumping this in case it got buried —
  still have one pilot slot open this month."
- **Touch 3 (day 7):** New angle, new value. Share one concrete tip they can
  use without you: "Quick win either way: calls between 6-8pm convert 2x —
  worth checking your voicemail log for that window."
- **Touch 4 (day 14):** The breakup. "Closing the loop — I'll stop bugging
  you. If missed calls ever become a headache, my number's below."

Each touch is a fresh email, not a reply-all chain. Keep the thread clean.

## What NOT to do

- Never attach files, flyers, or PDFs to a cold email. Spam filters eat them
  and nobody opens them. Link nothing in email 1.
- Never lie about how you found them ("we've been following your work" —
  you haven't).
- Never fake a prior relationship ("Re:", "Following up" on first contact).
- Never promise specific results you can't guarantee ("double your revenue").
- Keep lists clean: public business emails only, no personal addresses, no
  re-sending to people who said no, honor every unsubscribe immediately.
- If your open rate craters, stop and fix deliverability (SPF/DKIM/DMARC,
  warmed domain, plain-text format) before sending more volume.

## Handling replies

- **"How much?"** → "The pilot's free for 14 days. After that it's
  {price}/mo, cancel anytime. But let's see if it works for you first —
  10 minutes Tuesday?"
- **"How does it work?"** → Now you may reveal the mechanism, briefly, in
  plain language tied to their pain. Two sentences max, then back to the ask.
- **"Not interested."** → "No worries — thanks for the reply. Door's open
  if that changes." Then never email them again.
- **"Send me info."** → Send 3 bullet points, not a deck. End with the call
  ask again.

## Adapting the formula to other offers

The structure survives any product. Swap the variables:

- **Pain:** what costs them money or sleep weekly?
- **Proof:** smallest true claim you can make.
- **Offer:** what can you give away free for 14 days that proves value?
- **Ask:** what 10-minute commitment moves this forward?

Test one variable at a time: subject lines first (they gate everything),
then the hook, then the offer. Change nothing else while a test runs.

## Quick self-check before sending

- [ ] Under 120 words?
- [ ] First line references something specific about them?
- [ ] Zero mention of your tech/method in the hook?
- [ ] Exactly one call to action?
- [ ] No attachments, no links in touch 1?
- [ ] Would *you* reply to this?
